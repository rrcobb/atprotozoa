commonweal

An alternate governance kit. Found a commons with a charter link, let sortition draw a fresh council every round so power never settles, run a Venetian-doge-style lot-and-ballot draw for a rotating facilitator, and vote — with one seat always held by an AI delegate reading the room. Works the same whether the commons has 3 people or 200. Asked for by @shimmermathlabs.com.

why this works at 3 people and at 200

3-person co-op. Council size = everyone, always — sortition has nothing to narrow. The doge draw still runs (lot then ballot) to pick who chairs the meeting without it always being the same person. Concord sits in as a fourth, non-human seat: a tiebreaker and a devil's advocate, not a vote that can outweigh two humans agreeing.
200-person commons. A fresh council of ~25-28 is drawn for every round, seeded from the round tag — nobody can lobby their way onto next week's council because nobody, including the person who calls the round, knows who it'll be until the tag is typed. Any member can force a "full moot" vote of the whole 200 if a council decision looks captured or just deserves wider buy-in.

the council formula

Groups of 5 or fewer: everyone is the council. Above that: round(2·√n), floored at 5 and capped at 25 seats. It grows with the group so a council never gets it easy by staying tiny, but it stops growing once a room of 25 is already too big to have a real conversation in.

concord, the ai delegate

Concord doesn't call a model — no Workers AI, no API keys, nothing that costs anything to run. It's a small script that reads a proposal for cooperative language (share, consent, rotate, repair, transparent...) against extractive language (control, hoard, coerce, surveil, monopoly...) and leans yes, no, or abstain accordingly, with its reasoning always shown. Treat it as a seat that never gets tired of re-reading the fine print for whose interest it actually serves — not an oracle, and easy to overrule.
