Instead it's real, measured data: how often each word actually appears in PubMed's ~14M biomedical abstracts, year by year, from Kobak et al. 2025. Every line here is a real rate except one — add “wearing a trenchcoat” and watch it eat the chart. That one's still fake; there's no PubMed trend for a meme about a screenshot.

Methodology: each point is (abstracts containing the word that year) ÷ (total PubMed abstracts that year) × 10,000 — a real, citable rate, 2010 through 2024. Source: Kobak, González-Márquez, Horvát & Lause (2025), “Delving into LLM-assisted writing in biomedical publications through excess vocabulary,” Science Advances 11(27), eadt3813 — data and code on GitHub (MIT licensed). When you select more than one term the chart normalizes to the loudest one in the set, same as real Google Trends comparison mode — which is why “wearing a trenchcoat” flattens everything else the moment you add it. That one line is the exception: hand-drawn, not measured, because no one's PubMed abstract mentions a screenshot meme.
