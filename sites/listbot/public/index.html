listbot

Keep your own Bluesky lists by tagging. Sign up once; after that you never come back here — unless you want to edit something.

How it works

  1. Sign up below.
  2. See a post by someone you want on a list? Reply @listbot.bisks.net bots — or any list name you like.
  3. That post's author joins your list called "bots". If you don't have one, listbot makes it.
  4. @listbot.bisks.net remove bots takes them off again.

Bluesky already reads lists for feeds, starter packs, muting and blocking, so a list is useful the moment it exists.

You don't have to be terse

A tag goes to an agent that reads the thread, the person's profile, and the lists you already have. So you can just say what you mean:

  • @listbot.bisks.net do itworks when the context makes it obvious — someone who plainly belongs on one of your lists, or a thread where you were already talking about one.
  • @listbot.bisks.net add them to my cool posterslands on your existing "cool posters" list rather than making a near-duplicate.
  • @listbot.bisks.net make me a list for tracking ai news peopleas a normal post, replying to nobody. You get an empty list, ready to add to.
  • @listbot.bisks.net make me a mute list for crypto spammersa list you can actually point a mute or a block at — see below.

When it genuinely can't tell which list you meant, it asks instead of guessing.

Two kinds of list

Most lists are curation lists: they feed list-feeds and starter packs. That's the default, and it's what you get unless you say otherwise.

A moderation list is the only kind a mute or a block can point at, so ask for one if that's what you want — "make me a mute list for X". You can also switch a list over later on your lists page without losing anyone on it.

Worth knowing: muting and blocking are decisions each subscriber makes about a list, not properties of the list itself. One person's block list is another person's mute list. Your mutes are private; your blocks are public records.

Sign up

listbot asks for exactly two permissions: create, change and delete lists, and create and delete list memberships. It cannot post as you, follow, or read your DMs.

Already signed up? see and edit your lists.

Whose call is a list

Yours, entirely. The lists live in your own repo, signed by your own account, and mean whatever you decide they mean. listbot is a tool layer — it never judges whether a tag is "right", because there's nobody for it to be right for. If you put someone on a list called "bots" and they aren't a bot, that's between you and your list.

This is also why it's lists and not labels: a label is one labeler's signed assertion about someone, so it can't be per-person. A list can.

Plumbing

listbot holds an OAuth session so it can act on your PDS minutes after you tag it, with no browser open. Refresh tokens are encrypted at rest with a key held as a Worker secret, separate from the storage itself. You can revoke it any time from Bluesky's own app settings.

The agent that reads your tag can only read — it has no write tools and never holds your credentials. It returns a decision, and listbot's server does the writing. So the person it adds is always the author of the post you replied to, never someone named in the text of a tag.
