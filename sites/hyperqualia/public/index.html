what am I actually looking at?

A tesseract has 16 vertices at every combination of (±1, ±1, ±1, ±1) in x, y, z, w, and 32 edges connecting vertices that differ in exactly one coordinate. Every frame it's rotated simultaneously in the x–w plane and the y–z plane — a genuinely 4D motion with no 3D equivalent, which is most of why it reads as unreadable. The left panel projects that straight down to 2D (divide by distance-minus-w, then again by distance-minus-z) so you can watch the whole higher-dimensional object tumble.

The right panel is a real slice, not a stand-in: a hyperplane w = c(t) sweeps back and forth, and for each of the tesseract's 24 square faces, the two points where that face's edges cross the hyperplane are connected — the union of those segments is the exact 3D cross-section, assembled the way you'd do it on paper. Eighteen "hyperqualia inducers" (breathwork, ayahuasca, prayer, endogenous DMT, and the rest) sit at fixed points in the same 4D space and undergo the same rotation; when the sweeping plane's w value passes within reach of one, it appears in the cross-section and gets a line in the narrative below — the readable, linear residue of something that was never linear to begin with.
