how to play
  1. There is one breadboard with one 555, shared by both players. Each player has their own stack of parts; only you can place from yours.
  2. Take turns. On your turn do one thing: pick a part from your stack and click two holes to place it, or click any part already on the board (yours or your opponent's) to pick it up and click two new holes to move it (or "put it back" to cancel without spending your turn), or pass. Moving is how you advance your own circuit, or block theirs.
  3. The board has the 555's eight pins plus a V+ rail (top) and a GND rail (bottom). Wires join holes into one net. Wires and part labels are coloured by who placed them.
  4. Whoever's move makes the 555 oscillate wins. That takes a real astable: supply and ground on the chip, reset high, trigger tied to threshold, a capacitor to ground, and a resistor route that charges it. Two ways work (one uses pin 7 and two resistors, one feeds back from pin 3 through a single resistor).
  5. A diode and an inductor are in each stack to waste your turns. Short V+ to GND and you let the smoke out, so don't.
  6. Online games sync through an Automerge document. The join link carries its address, so reloading the page rejoins your seat.
